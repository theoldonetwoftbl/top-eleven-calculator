SKILLBEFOREAFTER
How it works, briefly. Each training session buys a pool of attribute points with the condition it costs. Those points are shared out between the drill's skills in a fixed rotation, with highlighted skills taking a double share. A drill gradually stops working as the average of its own skills climbs toward 180.
Two things worth knowing. A drill does not earn more points for training more skills — it splits the same pool further, so a 2-skill drill raises each of its skills far faster than a 5-skill one. And training is flat from age 18 to 21, then drops sharply: a 26-year-old trains at roughly 30% of a teenager's rate.
The full model, and where it's uncertain
The formula. points = 0.002064 × age_mult × level_mult × condition_spent × (180 − drill_average), where condition_spent = slots × intensity. Slot count, drill intensity and drill size all act through condition spent — they need no separate terms. Note that condition cost per session is flat at any level; what a higher drill average reduces is the points that session buys.
Age. ×1.0 for 18–21, then ×0.785 per year (95% CI 0.705–0.863). Measured on identical runs at ages 18, 19, 21, 23 and 26; beats a no-age model by ΔAIC = 28.6. Not a level effect in disguise — the 26-year-old had the lowest skills of any subject, where the model predicts it should have been fastest.
Rotation. One pass over every skill the drill trains, then a second pass over the highlighted ones. Verified exactly: on Carioca with Ladders the rotation predicted 26.00 and 13.00 points; the real log gave 26 and 13.
Condition. slots × intensity (Very Easy 0.75% · Easy 1.5% · Medium 2.25% · Hard 3% · Very Hard 3.75%), flat in level and age. Rest Packs restore 15%. A Campus condition drain boost cuts the condition drained but not the points: at −10% a 25-session run drained 0.89× the listed cost while gaining exactly what the listed cost predicts. Bigger boosts (−20% to −50%) are assumed to work the same way.
Drill level. The game says upgrading a drill adds 10% at Semi-Pro, 20% at Pro and 30% at World Class, and that holds up. One player trained 25 sessions at each level, so player-to-player differences cancel out. Measured: Semi-Pro ×1.11, Pro ×1.23, World Class ×1.31, each within half a standard error of the advertised value. The calculator uses the advertised 1.10 / 1.20 / 1.30. Level speeds up gains only: every run drained exactly the listed condition, so upgraded drills are not cheaper per session. (An earlier test that found only +4% at Semi-Pro is overturned. Its "Amateur" comparison run was most likely on an upgraded drill.)
Where it's weakest. Age 22 was never logged, so the model assumes the plateau ends at 21. Ages past 26 are extrapolated. Two 6-slot runs came in ~15% low (not statistically significant). Player-to-player variation of about ±10% is real and unexplained. Drill levels were measured on one player, and the level setting applies to every drill in the plan. The rate was fitted on six drills; the rest of the catalogue is an educated extension. Teamplay drills and Goalkeeper Training aren't included at all. Ranges shown are 10th–90th percentile, not guarantees.
